2     Abstract
Regional civic information systems combine municipal texts, agency notices and time-sensitive
observations, but software reuse can obscure which evidence and operating assumptions be-
long to a particular place. We present Davis CA Intel as a reproducible software-deployment
and corpus-description case study. A thin regional repository selects identity, source policy
and accepted public evidence, while Region Intel supplies shared acquisition, validation, search
and manuscript methods. The recorded Davis municipal edition contains 290 article pages and
2541 section records from eCode360 DA4922, verified at 2026-10-31T03:39:57.381Z. We derive
a complete article-level structural profile from that accepted snapshot and retain the exact nu-
merical table behind the figures. Configuration declares 10 sources and 8 enabled monitors;
these are not successful-collection counts. A pinned revision of docxology/template produces
the cover, graphical abstract, PDF and offline HTML, with source, artwork, derived-data and
rendering identities bound in the review packet. The contribution is an inspectable boundary
between reusable computation, local evidence and publication presentation. No civic-impact
experiment, human usability study, independent legal review or model-answer benchmark is
reported.


3     Introduction
3.1   Problem and scope
A useful regional information system must answer two different questions: what its software
can do, and what its retained evidence actually supports. A source adapter may be implemented
without a current observation. A searchable municipal snapshot may be internally coherent
without establishing that every provision remains current. A well-formed generated answer
may still require substantive review. These distinctions become harder to maintain when a sec-
ond locality inherits a first locality’s configuration, language and publication artifacts.
This case study concerns Davis, California. Its unit of observation is the accepted DA4922
municipal-code record set and the deployment documents that select its operating policy. It
examines whether shared software can preserve regional identity, reproduce a descriptive cor-
pus profile and publish a manuscript whose numerical statements remain attributable to that
record set. It does not estimate public benefit, legal completeness or hazard exposure. The origi-
nal Crescent City application supplies compatibility history in the shared package; its empirical
findings are not Davis observations.

3.2   Scholarship and design commitments
Open-government data scholarship cautions against treating the act of opening data as a suﬀi-
cient mechanism for realizing public value [Janssen et al., 2012]. For this project, the practical
implication is a research boundary: accessible records and working interfaces are intermediate
artifacts, whereas usefulness to residents requires separate empirical assessment.
PROV-DM distinguishes entities, activities and responsible agents [World Wide Web Consor-
tium, 2013]. We use that distinction to separate a retained municipal edition, the transforma-
tions applied to it and the ownership of the resulting packet. Our receipts are a project-specific
implementation of those concerns, not a claim of complete PROV serialization or formal confor-
mance. The W3C’s Data on the Web Best Practices adds an operational emphasis on provenance,
quality, version indicators and preservation [World Wide Web Consortium, 2017].
Dataset documentation offers a complementary perspective. Datasheets describe motivation,
composition, collection and intended uses [Gebru et al., 2021]; data statements make context

and limits of generalization explicit [Bender and Friedman, 2018]. The Davis claim ledger and
data statement adapt those questions to a municipal text deployment. They do not convert
administrative text into a representative sample of residents or describe a machine-learning

The manuscript makes three bounded contributions: an explicit shared/local ownership bound-
ary; a reproducible, complete description of article-level section counts in the accepted snap-
shot; and a publication workflow that uses the external template’s actual rendering APIs while
retaining input and output commitments. These are engineering and documentation contribu-
tions. Novel civic outcomes, legal interpretation and model capability are outside the reported
evidence.


4     Methods
4.1   Deployment identity and source admission
The versioned deployment document selects a civic profile, regional provider policy, output
directory, accepted-seed directory, enabled monitors and source catalog. The loader rejects
crossed profile identities before starting a producer. Davis uses municipality code DA4922.
Configured zones, routes and watch bounds are operating parameters, not surveyed city bound-
aries. Generic acquisition, artifact contracts, search, rendering and algorithm tests belong to
Region Intel. Davis owns its regional declarations, evidence and authored manuscript.
Acquisition, when separately undertaken, uses a candidate root rather than overwriting
accepted output. The accepted edition records article and section membership, local extrac-
tion/binding checks, a current table-of-contents comparison and a bounded deterministic
live sample. A sample pass is not an all-page currentness claim. Source-marked reserved or
repealed sections can remain legitimate retained records when the corresponding heading is
present; an unmarked empty extraction is a different state. Failed or interrupted candidates
retain their evidence instead of replacing the accepted edition.
This paper reads the tracked accepted public seed. It does not rerun acquisition or substitute
whichever live producer output is newest. Its verification clock therefore remains 2026-10-
31T03:39:57.381Z through later figure generation and rendering. Raw acquisition diagnostics
remain local; public commitments identify their recorded role without making omitted source
files reconstructible from a hash.

4.2   Descriptive corpus profile
Let the accepted article-page set be 𝐴, with 𝑛 = |𝐴|, and let 𝑐𝑖 be the number of retained section
objects in article page 𝑖. The total section-record count is 𝑁 = ∑𝑖 𝑐𝑖 . The shared profiler
computes these quantities and the minimum, median and maximum of the complete vector of
counts. Duplicate article identifiers or malformed article structure fail admission.
The histogram includes every accepted article page, including a zero count if present. The com-
panion horizontal bars display at most the twelve article pages with the largest counts; ties are
resolved by article identifier and GUID. The exact CSV retains every row, so a reader can inspect
the complete distribution rather than infer it from the selected bars. The histogram uses at most
25 equal-width bins, bounded by the observed range. No resampling, population inference,
significance test or uncertainty interval is applied: these are descriptive properties of a finite
retained snapshot.
Counts describe publisher/extractor segmentation. They are not measures of policy impor-
tance, regulatory burden, textual diﬀiculty or neighborhood experience. Article titles and num-

bers help a reader locate records; the analysis does not infer legal topics or assign normative
weights.

4.3   Evidence projection and figure production
Required and optional seed members are captured through bounded, no-follow reads into a pri-
vate snapshot before the existing publication validator parses them. Counts must agree with the
accepted verification report. The manuscript also captures deployment/configuration bytes, the
immutable shared dependency and frozen lock, bibliography, claim ledger, numbered sources
and cover image. The effective selected configuration participates even when a caller explicitly
selects an alternative deployment document.
A composite renderer fingerprint binds the shared input, projection, membership, template-
adapter and figure-generation modules, together with the pinned template asset inventory.
Evidence tokens hydrate prose from those captured inputs. Matplotlib produces the measured
corpus plot and two explicitly conceptual method diagrams as PNG, PDF and SVG. The CSV
and profile JSON remain available alongside them. The cover is an AI-generated editorial illus-
tration; it is not a map, observation or analytical figure.

4.4   Template-backed publication
The native backend is docxology/template at revision c18dae54a07fe6ac3a7b1a1a274251c169a12e7a.
The shared adapter verifies the checkout’s identity, commit and tracked cleanliness and verifies
that the imported rendering module belongs to that checkout. It calls the actual combined PDF
and HTML RenderManager APIs. The template supplies title/cover composition, graphical
front matter, typography, citations and offline reader assets. Direct Pandoc invocation is not
an alternative regional backend.
Native work occurs in a fresh ignored namespace under the selected template checkout because
its offline asset writer confines outputs to that root. The adapter copies only declared publi-
cation members to the regional staging directory. The template source remains unchanged.
Source inputs are checked again before recoverable promotion; failed staging and the previous
complete edition remain available. Rendering behavior follows reproducibility principles that
connect reported results to the code and data that produced them [Sandve et al., 2013], while
making the rendering toolchain an explicit dependency.

5.3   Reusable boundary and public surfaces
The local dashboard and static edition use the deployment’s accepted evidence and oﬀicial
source links. A successful source search is distinguishable from a model-generated answer.
Retrieval-augmented generation combines retrieval with a generative model [Lewis et al., 2020],
but that architectural relationship does not establish the factuality of this deployment’s gener-
ated answers. No model benchmark or answer-quality result is presented here.
The distinction illustrated in fig. 3 is an explicit ownership and reporting boundary. Software
acceptance and native artifact checks are retained in the review record rather than interpreted
as measurements of civic usefulness. The rendered packet contains the template identity, input
fingerprint, complete offline asset inventory and output hashes. Those commitments make a
candidate inspectable; they do not certify its interpretation.

6     Discussion
6.1   What this case establishes
The Davis deployment demonstrates an implemented separation between shared computation
and local evidence. A regional consumer can retain its municipality, provider policy, accepted
seed and scholarly narrative while importing a common runner and manuscript pipeline. That
separation reduces opportunities to relabel another locality’s records, although it does not elim-
inate the need for source review or prove generality across every supported adapter.
The corpus profile adds a modest but reproducible empirical description. Publishing its com-
plete numerical table makes the chart auditable and makes the article/section denominator ex-
plicit. It does not turn a municipal-code snapshot into a comprehensive account of governance.
A record absent from this corpus might be outside the publisher’s organization, outside the re-
tained edition or unavailable to the acquisition procedure; the present data cannot distinguish
every such explanation.
FAIR principles distinguish findability, accessibility, interoperability and reuse [Wilkinson et al.,
2016]. Stable identities, public descriptive metadata and explicit source-use boundaries address
some related engineering concerns here. However, this work does not perform a FAIR maturity
assessment or claim that public accessibility grants unrestricted redistribution rights. An exact
hash helps identify an artifact; it does not settle permissions or meaning.

6.2   Portability without semantic substitution
The reusable unit is a computation and its declared contract, not a place name or a claimed
outcome. Source access, oﬀicial geographic identifiers, rights, observation clocks and intended
uses must be reviewed for each locality. Separating configuration from implemented methods
makes that review easier to locate. It cannot supply a missing provider, community perspective
or institutional mandate.
Dataset documentation practices motivate this emphasis on context [Gebru et al., 2021, Bender
and Friedman, 2018]. In a regional setting, the unit being documented is an administrative-

text snapshot and its deployment policy. It would be a category error to infer demographic
representativeness, accessibility to residents or public trust from that documentation alone.

6.3   Research agenda
Future evaluation should begin with named user tasks and appropriate expertise. Examples
include locating an oﬀicial code provision, distinguishing a source update from a stale cached
record, and recognizing when the interface lacks enough evidence to answer. Outcomes could
include task completion, citation correctness, recognition of uncertainty and accessibility bar-
riers. Any study requires a declared sample, protocol, baseline and ethics/consent decisions
suited to its participants.
A legal-answer evaluation would need expert-adjudicated questions, jurisdiction and edition
controls, an explicit definition of supported versus unsupported answers, and separate scoring
of citations and interpretation. Dahl and colleagues document legal hallucinations in the mod-
els and tasks they studied [Dahl et al., 2024]; their rates are not Davis measurements and are not
imported as a forecast for this system. Their relevance is the need for task-specific evaluation
rather than an assumption that fluent retrieval-grounded text is legally correct.
The NIST AI Risk Management Framework separates governance, context mapping, measure-
ment and management [National Institute of Standards and Technology, 2023]. The present
artifact controls contribute to an inspectable engineering record, but they do not establish a
completed risk-management program or a trustworthiness certification.


7     Limitations, Ethics, and Threats to Validity
Time and completeness. Local hash agreement concerns retained bytes. A table-of-contents
check and deterministic sample at acquisition time do not prove continuing all-page current-
ness, comprehensive city communications or legal completeness. Rendering never advances
the source-verification clock. Changed upstream pages can invalidate an interpretation while
leaving the historical snapshot perfectly reproducible.
Segmentation and interpretation. Articles and sections are operational publisher/extractor
units. Larger counts do not imply greater legal importance, regulatory burden or community
attention. Reserved or repealed units can remain meaningful records. The profile does not
resolve amendment history, effective dates, cross-references or legal interpretation beyond the
accepted source contract.
Selection and geography. The configured catalog is a reviewed selection, not an exhaustive
list of institutions or events. Zones, watch boxes and radii are adapter settings, not authorita-
tive municipal boundaries. Missing, unavailable, stale and empty observations remain distinct
states. An accessible code corpus cannot supply resident experience, neighborhood-level con-
ditions or the views of groups absent from administrative records.
Models and automation. No model-generated answer is evaluated. A retrieval result, citation
link or successful model invocation is not a demonstration of legal accuracy. General legal-AI
findings motivate caution but do not establish this system’s error rate. Human expert review
remains necessary for any claimed legal interpretation or answer-quality result.
Artwork and visual rhetoric. The cover is an original AI-generated conceptual illustration. It
is not a photograph, oﬀicial city branding, surveyed map or depiction of a measured event. The
architecture and evidence diagrams are explanatory designs. Only the corpus-profile figure
reports measured counts, with an exact numerical fallback. These roles are stated so that visual
polish cannot substitute for evidence.

8   Reproducibility and Artifact Specification
The consumer installs its full public Region Intel commit through a frozen Bun lockfile. Shared
algorithms and publication methods remain in that dependency. A sibling source checkout is
not required for normal consumer commands. Native manuscript rendering additionally re-
quires the pinned external docxology/template checkout and its prepared Python/native tools.

bun install --frozen-lockfile
bun run validate
bun run manuscript:check
bun run manuscript:render
bun run manuscript:validate-render
bun run manuscript:bundle
bun run manuscript:validate-render -- --bundle

The documented environment selects the template root and interpreter explicitly. The shared
adapter rejects a wrong repository, commit or modified tracked source. It records actual tem-
plate, Python, Matplotlib, Pandoc and TeX versions; the completed native packet is also checked
with qpdf and pdftotext. The template’s combined writers generate the manuscript; regional
code does not silently substitute a separate direct-Pandoc rendering path.
The accepted seed, effective configuration, package/lock, numbered sources, bibliography,
claim ledger and cover bytes bind the manuscript fingerprint. That fingerprint also binds the
shared adapter/projection/figure source and pinned web-asset inventory. The article-level
CSV and profile JSON are exact derived tables. PDF, SVG and PNG variants preserve the
distinction between measured counts and explanatory diagrams.
A complete HTML edition includes its assets and figure directory. Copying only the HTML
entry point omits font, mathematics and image dependencies. The review validator checks
bounded regular files, exact member inventory, current source projection, output hashes and
pinned template asset hashes. These checks are consistent with the W3C emphasis on version
and preservation metadata [World Wide Web Consortium, 2017], but successful replay is not a
proof of semantic correctness.
The reproducibility record distinguishes computational repeatability, native rendering and sub-
stantive acceptance. Bitwise PDF identity across different native toolchains is not promised:
versions, timestamps and font machinery can affect serialization. Rebuilding a changed candi-
date requires renewed source, figure, page and applicable independent review. Previous failed
work and the last complete packet remain available rather than being overwritten with a partial
artifact.
Data and source are available in the public Davis repository. Raw source acquisition and oper-
ational diagnostics remain local. A public digest identifies omitted evidence; it is not a replace-
ment for that evidence and does not allow a reader to reconstruct it.

Before committing, the operator stages the intended public files and checks the index. After
committing, the second command compares the current commit with public input and complete
packet bytes:

# After staging, before committing

# After committing
bun run publication:check

This shared preflight rejects missing, ignored, linked or changed required Git members. It does
not stage files, publish a release or certify source interpretation.


9      Conclusion
Davis CA Intel provides an implemented thin regional consumer with its own identity, accepted
municipal edition and scholarly manuscript. Shared methods derive a 290-article, 2541-section
structural profile from retained evidence and pass the hydrated sources to a pinned external
template for native publication rendering. Exact tables, asset identities and source/output com-
mitments make the case inspectable.
The study’s contribution is a reproducible boundary between shared computation, regional ev-
idence and presentation. It does not establish ongoing legal currency, legal correctness, usabil-
ity, civic impact or model factuality. Those questions require their own methods and evidence
before a 0.1 publication decision.
